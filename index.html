Novel-view render of the Panama reef by PEARL.

Water eats red light first, veils the distance in blue, and quietly convinces 3D Gaussian Splatting to fill the sea with floaters.

Underwater, the colour a camera records depends on how far the light travelled. Absorption and backscatter change with range, wavelength and viewing direction, so the same coral looks different from every view. Standard 3DGS treats those changes as geometry: it spends primitives on empty water and under-reconstructs the thin branches that matter.

PEARL is a medium-aware training framework that fixes both sides of the problem. It decides where Gaussians go by following the error that is left after the water is modelled, under a hard cap on how many may exist. And it decides what they learn by tying appearance to the Beer–Lambert physics of light in water.

Two principles, one training loop

Principle A

Spend Gaussians where the error lives

Every pixel's residual is attributed back to the Gaussians that drew it, by their compositing responsibility, and smoothed over time. Primitives that are both used and persistently wrong score highest. Splits and clones are taken in score order until the budget is full; the least useful primitives are pruned to make room.

  • Gradient activity reweighted by a responsibility-weighted residual EMA
  • Hard primitive cap enforced at every refinement round
  • Principal-axis splitting and complexity-guided seeding
Principle B

Supervise with the physics of water

Each Gaussian's colour is attenuated by its own distance along the ray, then a backscatter veil is added up to the rendered depth. Training compares that medium-aware render to the photo with a relative L1 and SSIM loss, weights coral edges more, and keeps the learned attenuation smooth and red-first.

  • View-conditioned medium network for colour, backscatter and attenuation
  • Sobel edge weighting for thin structures
  • Beer–Lambert regulariser: magnitude, smoothness, R ≥ G ≥ B

Four reefs, rendered from new viewpoints

Over its 3DGS backbone, PEARL gains +3.61 dB PSNR and cuts LPIPS by 25%, with the best SSIM and LPIPS on all four SeaThru-NeRF scenes, under a hard budget of one million Gaussians.

Capacity is the lever

On Curaçao, raising the budget from 10K to 1M Gaussians moves quality far more than swapping the densification heuristic does. Uncapped training on IUI3 grew past 35M primitives and exhausted an 80 GB GPU. PEARL treats the cap as a hard constraint and spends it well.

Memory fixed in advance

Because the count is bounded, so is rendering memory, and it is known before training starts. On Curaçao, PEARL renders in less memory than WaterSplatting at comparable capacity.

Not only underwater

On sparse-view LLFF Fern, where supervision is limited by view count rather than by water, the same recipe reaches the highest SSIM among the compared methods.
